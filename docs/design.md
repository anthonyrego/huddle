# huddle: how it works

This is the detail behind AGENTS.md: every package in full, how the voice and video paths work and why, the ears, and what the models were found to do. Before changing a part, read its section here. The history of each step and its measurements are in `history.md`.

## Packages

### `internal/wire`
What the app and the server say to each other: WebSocket envelopes (`hello`, `welcome`, `board.version`, `board.ops`, `agent.status`, `command.typed`, `command.address`, `transcript.segment`, `error`) and the dev join (`DevJoin`, whose `Bot` makes an identity ending in `BotSuffix`; `Joined` names the media server: `MediaURL`, huddled's own `/rtc`, and `Media`, always `builtin`, so an app refuses a server from before huddled had its own). `Protocol` is checked on hello. Its `signal` package is the /rtc protocol of huddle's SFU (join, the two connections' offers and candidates, tracks, a viewer's `settings`, and `wanted`, which tells a publisher the layers of its video anyone is sent: Dynacast). Pure.

### `internal/rtc`
The app's side of the media, as an interface: `Connector`, `Room`, `Mic`, `Speaker`, events. Audio crosses it as 48 kHz mono float32 in 10 ms frames. Pure.

### `internal/rtc/room`
`rtc.Connector` over a media server. What it says to the server goes through a private transport (`transport.go`: participants; the tracks it's sent, with their RTCP and the size, on/off and PLI asked of a video; publishing the mic and a simulcast video with each layer's RTCP; the estimator on the connection it publishes on; leaving), `sfu.go`, on `sfuclient.Session` (huddle's own SFU: the room's send interceptors, then `icept.NewCC` alone, on the publish connection, its estimator handed over inside Join; the receive ones on the other; `SendOnly` subscribes to none; participants with the agent flag; each track's codec from what was negotiated, since pion announces a track before its first packet with no codec yet; its RTCP read and handed to the room; a video's size and on/off as `signal.Settings` and its PLI on the receive connection; a simulcast video's layers as RIDs f, h, q (f and q for two), the smallest the transceiver's, and the SFU's `wanted` for it turned back into layers and handed to the video (`wantedOut`, a hook beside `videoOut`); a server that can't be reached reads "could not reach the media server: dial tcp …", one on loopback gets loopback candidates; no resume, so reconnecting never fires and the app rejoins on disconnected; tested against the real SFU on loopback in `sfu_test.go`). Publishes the mic as Opus with the RFC 6464 level on every packet (the SFU's speaker detection reads it) and the RTP timestamp following the mic (DTX and mute read as time going by; the first DTX frame of a silence is sent, then one every 400 ms); reads each remote audio track as raw RTP into its own jitter buffer; mixes them with `playout`. Logs each track's numbers every 10 s (`voice: track`). `Connector.Sim` makes the receive side worse through a pion interceptor (`icept.Sim`). Video (see Video, below): `PublishVideo` sends each layer of an `rtc.VideoSource` as one layer of a simulcast track (H.264 Constrained Baseline), the capture time as the RTP timestamp, a receiver's PLI or FIR to that layer's encoder at most every 200 ms; congestion control is pion's GCC with our pacer (`icept`: the voice at once, video at 1.2× the estimate the layers were given from, the cap, never less than what they were given) and an allocator (`alloc.go`: the estimate capped at 1.5× what was sent, shared lowest layer first, paused layers probed for by padding the highest running one with SEI filler payloads; Dynacast: a layer nobody is sent gets nothing, which is neither a failed try nor a pause of the layers above it, a wanted layer that isn't running has the next smaller one stand in for it, a layer paused for nobody wanting it comes back at its floor at once if it ran at its floor or more, GCC's estimate leaves 1.15× that and hasn't dropped since, else by a probe carried by the highest layer needed, a layer wanted under a larger one of its video that runs starts at its floor at once if GCC's estimate leaves 1.15× it (waiting, it paused the larger one), and with nothing running to carry a probe the lowest layer needed starts at its floor once its hold is over; a change of `wanted` shares the estimate out at once; `video: sending` says which layers are wanted and why each paused one is, `unwanted` or `bandwidth`, and `video: layer` why each starts or stops); `Connector.Uplink` narrows what's sent (`icept.Uplink`), and `Connector.Downlink` what's received (`icept.Downlink`, ahead of everything on the receive connections; `DownlinkFor` lifts it). Remote video: an access-unit assembler (`assemble.go`: a unit ends at its marker or where the next begins, at a new timestamp or a keyframe's first packet, and a unit cut off so is dropped; it starts, and picks up after a hole it gives up on, at a keyframe's first packet; a hole is given up 250 ms after it showed, not after the assembler got to it; while a keyframe is awaited, a frame a packet shows isn't one is dropped at once, holes and filler too, and the keyframe is asked for when the hole is given up; padding-only packets, the SFU's probes between frames and its stand-ins for a publisher's filler, take their sequence numbers and carry nothing, a stand-in's marker ending its frame), the app's decoder (`Connector.NewDecoder`), only frames newer than the last shown shown, a `video.Latest` per track, lip sync against the same person's voice by arrivals (each track's `srClock`: when each RTP timestamp would have come with the least delay of the last 2–4 s; sender reports only while a track has no packets) (`videorecv.go`); `SetSize` throttled to 4 a second, (0, 0) turns the track off. `Connector.VideoFrame` hears every frame shown (with when its last packet came, when it was put together, and how it was synced); `VideoPacket` and `VideoUnit` every packet read and unit put together (`huddle video -trace`); `VideoSendStats` and `VideoTracks` are the numbers tools print. Pure.

### `internal/rtc/fileio`
A WAV mic and a recording speaker, paced on the wall clock 10 ms a frame, for runs with no devices. Pure.

### `internal/rtc/codecs`
What every media connection speaks, client and SFU: Opus (PT 111, FEC, DTX) and H.264 Constrained Baseline (PT 102), their RTCP feedback (nack, pli, fir, transport-cc; none on audio), and the header extensions (transport-cc on video, the audio level on audio, mid and rid on video). The voice carries no transport-wide numbers: they're given as packets are written, the pacer sends the voice ahead of the video it holds, and pion's GCC took the departures running backwards for congestion. No RTX: pion's NACK responder never writes repaired-rid, so a retransmission goes out on the stream itself. Pure.

### `internal/rtc/icept`
pion interceptors either end's connections use: congestion control for what a connection sends (`pacer.go`: `NewCC` makes pion's GCC with our `Pacer` per connection and hands over each `Estimator`; the pacer sends the voice the moment it's written and paces video at 1.2× the rate it's given (`SetRate`), not at GCC's estimate, which climbs while less is sent), `Uplink` (a narrow uplink for tests: one token bucket at a fixed rate with a 50-packet queue in front, the tail dropped; next to the network, so the far end sees queueing as delay), `Downlink` (its mirror on the receive side: one queue per connection, every stream it receives drained into it and handed to its reader as the link lets it through, so the TWCC feedback after it sees the queue as delay; `RecvLink.SetRate(0)` lifts it; replay protection must be off, as for `Sim`) and `Sim` (a `netsim.Sim` on each remote stream, ahead of everything but `Downlink`; each packet carries when it came off the network, which `Arrived` reads from the attributes `ReadRTP` returns). Pure.

### `internal/rtc/sfuclient`
Joins huddle's own SFU (adapted from LiveKit's Go SDK, Apache 2.0). `Join(ctx, url, token, Options, Events)` (`session.go`): the /rtc WebSocket (join, then joined or the server's refusal; pings both ways), the two connections (`pc.go`: Pub, which the client offers in a 20 ms window and again after an answer if anything changed meanwhile; Sub, which answers the server's offers; candidates trickle both ways, the server's held until there's a remote description), the participant table, and `Events` in order on one goroutine of its own: Joined (may come before Join returns), Arrived, Left, TrackInfo, TrackAdded (a `RemoteTrack`: the `signal.TrackInfo`, pion's `TrackRemote` and `RTPReceiver`, once OnTrack fires, before the first packet; the caller reads its RTP and RTCP), TrackRemoved, Speakers, Wanted (the server's `signal.Wanted` for a video the client publishes: the layers anyone is sent, the rest to pause; every layer is wanted until the first), Connection, Disconnected (bye, `ErrBye`; the socket gone; a connection failed; never after `Close`). `publish.go`: `Publish(ctx, signal.Publish, tracks...)` sends publish before the transceiver goes on the connection (so no offer carries a track the server hasn't heard of), waits for the SID, then offers; a `published` that comes after the publish gave up (its context, the 10 s wait, or a server error that fails every waiter), or between giving up and forgetting it, is unpublished, so no track is left on the server; a video's layers are one `Track` each on one transceiver; `PublishAudio` (the mic: a `Track` and its SID; frames before it's negotiated are `ErrNotBound`), `Unpublish`, `Mute`; `subscribe.go`: `Settings` (a viewer's size and on/off for a video it's sent: the SFU sends nothing until the first, or 250 ms after the track is on the connection, then the top layer) and `PLI` (on the receive connection, for a track's SSRC); `Close`. A refused join is a `RefusedError` (its reason). `NewAPI` (on the publish connection the caller's interceptors, then transport-wide sequence numbers, pion's NACK responder outside them so a resend gets its own, and sender reports: congestion control in `Options.Publish` gets the publish connection's estimator from `OnNewPeerConnection`, once, inside Join, and never adds the transport-wide numbers again; on the receive connection pion's NACK generator every 20 ms, receiver reports and TWCC feedback, tracks announced before their first packet; `Options.NoReplayProtection` turns SRTP's replay protection off there, for a network simulation in `Subscribe`: it drops after SRTP, and without RTX the SFU's resend of a dropped packet has the same stream and sequence number, which SRTP takes for a replay) and `Track` (a mic or one simulcast layer: frames in at the caller's RTP timestamps, packets of at most 1200 bytes, the RFC 6464 level on audio, mid and rid on every packet until a receiver report names the SSRC, every RTCP packet about it to a handler). Tested end to end in `internal/server/sfu`, and under the room in `internal/rtc/room`. Pure.

### `internal/opus`
Opus at 48 kHz mono, 20 ms frames, in-band FEC, DTX: one `Encoder`/`Decoder` interface over libopus 1.6 transpiled to Go (`kazzmir/opus-go`, the one used) and `pion/opus`'s decoder. Every call into the transpiled code runs on one pinned worker goroutine (`pin.go`; see Rules). `PacketSamples` reads a packet's length. Pure.

### `internal/jitter`
One remote track's jitter buffer: reorders by sequence number; target delay = 95th percentile of arrival delay over the last 250 arrivals plus a frame, 40–300 ms, measured afresh after a second with no packets (see Voice); FEC from the next packet, else libopus concealment; late packets dropped; DTX silence played and the part past the target skipped; a burst that leaves more than `MaxDelay` plus 100 ms (400 ms) held trimmed back to the target; `Stats`; `Playing` (where the last pull of the stream itself began, counted back from where it ended, for lip sync). Pure.

### `internal/netsim`
Loss, jitter (uniform 0–2J, in order) and reordering, seedable (`Sim.Fate`) or in real time (`Pipe`). Pure.

### `internal/playout`
Mixes every track into 10 ms frames, paced by the speaker's blocking `Write` (the device's clock), soft-limited; per-participant levels for talking dots; follows each buffer's target by skipping a quiet frame or playing silence in place of one (never during speech, at most one frame in five). Pure.

### `internal/board`
The board: nodes (with kinds), links, groups, notes under boxes, the meeting's notes panel, sorts (`order`). Ops are the tool calls; `Apply` checks an op and applies it only if valid, with errors written for the model; `Replay` rebuilds a board from ops; `Doc.Ops` re-emits a board as the ops that rebuild it, and that `Snapshot` is a version's `Doc` on the wire and in the store; `ReadOps` reads fixtures; `Digest` is what `get_board` returns and every turn carries; `Tools` declares the tools (their descriptions are the model's manual), including the ones the server handles itself: `undo`, `revert_to`, `speak`, `transcript`, `auto`. Pure.

### `internal/board/layout`
`Layout`: a left-to-right flow with several roots and an optional sort key, seeded from the previous layout so an add moves as little as possible. `Board`: the whole board as a JSON `Placement` (boxes, frames, arrows, the notes panel, centers that seed the next layout): each group laid out on its own (a flow, or a list in columns) and kept together as one block, the parts no arrow joins packed in rows, the notes panel to the right. The server runs it for every version; an app runs it only when a version has no placement. Pure.

### `internal/board/measure`
Sizes cards, line breaks, frame labels and the notes panel with the embedded fonts only (`text.NewEmbeddedShaper`, no system fallback), so the server and every app agree; `Sizer` caches by content; `Place` is the server's layout. Pure.

### `internal/gate`
Whether someone is talking to the whiteboard: the name (fuzzy) at the start of an utterance or as a vocative, typed commands, the push-to-address key; where a command ends; the name ignored while the whiteboard speaks, except "stop". Deterministic, tested on transcript fixtures. Pure.

### `internal/server/agent`
The whiteboard, one per active room (`Manager`): joins the room's media as an agent participant through `Config.Media` (`media.go`: `Join` tells a `Hearing` who arrives and leaves and hands it each person's voice as a `VoiceTrack`, which a `*webrtc.TrackRemote` and an `sfu.Voice` are; the `MediaRoom` publishes the whiteboard's voice, counts people and leaves; `Builtin` in `sfuroom.go`, huddled's own SFU joined in-process through `sfu.LocalJoin`: no socket, no connections, its voice's frames straight into its publication); ears (`ears.go`: pion/opus decode at 16 kHz, `pcm.Detector`, a `gemini-3.5-transcribe-live` session per person, rotated before ten minutes); the gate; one `gemini-3.8-live` brain per room with a reconnect loop on GoAway (resumption handle in `agent_state`, compression on); a queue (one command in flight, in the order begun, auto turns last; a turn ended by no reply or timeout leaves the brain owing its turn-complete, and the next turn-complete before anything of the next turn's own is that one, not the next turn's end); versions committed per message of tool calls (`versions.go`, redone on `ErrHeadMoved`); a rolling summary (`gemini-3.5-flash-lite`); voice out (`voice.go`: 20 ms frames through `internal/opus`, FEC on, DTX off, behind an `Encoder` interface). `Trace` reports everything it does. It hears voices only (the SFU hands an in-process participant people's voices alone), so the SFU never sends it video; `TestAudioOnly` checks that in-process in CI, that the voice is handed over and the person counted, that the whiteboard's voice reaches the person and that their leaving ends their voice, timing each frame from the publisher's write to the whiteboard's read. Pure.

### `internal/fixturemeet`
Fixture meetings: script parsing (labels: command, question, stop, distractor), rendering with macOS `say` and ffmpeg, and bots that join a room through the dev join (as bots) and play their track. `JoinMic` (`mic.go`) publishes a bot's microphone on the media server the join names: a send-only `sfuclient` session, returned once the microphone is connected (frames before it's negotiated dropped, a session gone an error). `Track` is a mono Ogg/Opus file's packets with each one's RFC 6464 level, played in real time onto a `FrameWriter` (`WriteFrame(frame, ts, level)`, which `sfuclient.Track` and `sfu.LocalTrack` are; also what `huddled bot play` plays). Pure (it runs `say` and `ffmpeg`).

### `internal/devjoin`
The dev join's client side, for the app, the tools and the bots: `Join` (a server that can't be reached gives the `*url.Error`, a refusal "join: status: message", one whose media server isn't huddled's own an error saying so) and `RTC`, a join's answer as an `rtc.Join` (`MediaURL`, the token, the identity). Pure.

### `internal/cue`
Holds ops until the audio that came before them has been heard, one per gap. Copied from an earlier project; nothing uses it yet (boardview spaces its own reveal). Pure.

### `internal/live`
One Gemini Live session (`google.golang.org/genai`): a writer goroutine owns every send, a reader turns server messages into ordered `Event`s. Audio or text replies (`Config.Text`), interim and final input transcripts, `SendTurn` (client content), `SendText` (realtime text), `SendAudioEnd`. Pure.

### `internal/pcm`
Mono s16 helpers: conversions, dBFS levels, the speech `Detector`; float32 conversions and levels, `Correlation`, `BestLag`, `Segmental` (scores speech whose delay moves), click trains and `Onsets`. Pure.

### `internal/server/store`
Postgres through pgx: embedded, append-only migrations (`migrations/NNNN_name.sql`, run under an advisory lock); meetings; immutable board versions with `Commit(expectHead)` (`ErrHeadMoved` when another commit got there first); the ops of each version; transcript segments; the whiteboard's resumption state and rolling summary; the meeting's voice policy (`Voice`, `SetVoice`, in `meetings.settings`). Pure.

### `internal/server/wshub`
The meeting WebSocket: hello (checked against `wire.Protocol`), then the welcome and the head version, then broadcasts; what apps send goes to `OnMessage`. A client that can't keep up is dropped. Pure.

### `internal/server/httpapi`
`GET /healthz`, `POST /dev/join` (dev mode only, for any name in any room: the SFU at `ws(s)://<host>/rtc` and an `rtctoken` signed by `Server.Minter`; the meeting WebSocket's URL; a bot's identity ends in `-bot`), `GET /ws`, `GET /rtc` (the SFU's signalling, when `Server.RTC` is set). Pure.

### `internal/server/sfu`
The media server inside `huddled serve` (adapted from LiveKit's where noted, Apache 2.0). `Server` (`server.go`): `New(Config{Network, Tokens, Log})` opens the media port; `ServeHTTP` is /rtc (the join within 10 s, its `rtctoken` and `signal.Protocol` checked, else an error and a close; then pings every 15 s, a 1 MB read limit, a client whose 256-message queue fills dropped); `Close` tells everyone bye. Rooms (`room.go`) are made on the first join and gone when empty; a second session under one identity ends the first with bye; messages are queued under the room's lock, so everyone hears changes in order (a participant before its tracks, `track` before the offer that carries it, `untrack` before `left`). Each person has two connections (`transport.go`): up, which the client offers and the SFU answers; down, which the SFU offers with the down tracks the room says the person should have (`AddTrack`, so a removed track's m-line is used again), in a 20 ms window and again after the answer when something changed meanwhile, and an offer unanswered in 15 s ends the session with bye; candidates trickle both ways and wait for a remote description. An in-process participant (`local.go`, the whiteboard): `LocalJoin(rtctoken.Grant, LocalEvents)` puts one in a room with neither socket nor connections. Others see an ordinary participant (`Agent` as granted) and its voice as an ordinary publication: `PublishAudio` gives a `LocalTrack` whose `WriteFrame` (a `fixturemeet.FrameWriter`) stamps an SSRC and sequence numbers, forwards as a remote voice's packets are, and stands each frame as the publisher's sender report (RTP time ts is now), so down tracks send translated reports for it. It hears people, never agents (`LocalEvents`: Arrived, Left, Voice, in order on a goroutine of its own, never under the room's lock; the room's messages reach it through `participant.send` as they are), and is handed each person's voice, never a video or an agent's voice (`participant.wants`), as a `Voice`: a down track whose packets (its own copies, numbered and timed as on a connection, no header extensions) go to a queue of 250 (5 s; a reader behind loses the oldest, so nothing waits on it) instead of a connection, with `ReadRTP` (`os.ErrDeadlineExceeded` past the deadline, `io.EOF` after the end), `SetReadDeadline` and `Codec`, as `agent.VoiceTrack` wants. A voice ends when its publisher unpublishes or leaves; `People` counts people; `Leave`, a same-identity join or the server closing ends it. A publish (`publication.go`) gets its SID and is announced and subscribed at once (the client offers only after `published`); its TrackRemote is matched by ID to the publish's CID; a voice's packets go to every down track as they arrive, its sender reports are kept (those counting no packets dropped: pion makes up their RTP time). A video's layers (`layers.go`, largest first as published) are each read from their own stream by RID, their sender reports kept, and every packet goes to every down track; a layer that sends nothing for 300 ms while another of the track sends counts as paused (the room's clock checks), and its first packet after has it running; a keyframe is asked of a layer with a PLI on the publisher's up connection, at most one per layer per 200 ms whoever asks. A down track (`downtrack.go`) is our own `TrackLocal` (ID the SID, stream ID the publisher) written through `WriteStream` with the subscriber's payload type and SSRC, sequence numbers that run on unbroken (a new SSRC under the publication starts a run carrying on from the last, timestamps moved on by the time between; a publisher's padding alone isn't sent, and the SFU's own goes on a video only: the downlink's probes, and its stand-ins for a probe's filler), and only the subscriber's header extensions (the audio level under its ID); nothing before the publisher sends or before the subscriber's connection is up; its own sender reports, NTP the SFU's now and RTP the publisher's last report's moved on by the time since it arrived (a video's: the layer being sent's), one a second and one at once with the first packet or the publisher's first report. A video's down track (`downvideo.go`) runs a forwarder onto its one SSRC: the layer is the smallest at least the size the viewer's `settings` ask for (the largest for a bigger ask), or the nearest running one while that's paused (smaller first); off (`Enabled` false, or 0×0) sends nothing; nothing until the first settings, or the top layer 250 ms after the track went on the connection with none; settings for a track not sent are ignored (an error would fail a publish the client waits on). A start, a size, turned back on or a layer paused asks the new layer for a keyframe at once, and its packets ask again until its keyframe begins (a layer starting again isn't asked at once: a new encoder opens with one); a viewer's PLI or FIR goes to the layer it's sent; its NACKs are answered by pion's responder from what was sent, across switches. Dynacast (`dynacast.go`): a video's wanted set is, across its down tracks, the layer each viewer's settings chose (the top one after 250 ms with none; none while off), the smaller layer its downlink allows while that's limited, and the layer its forwarder sends (until a switch completes); its publisher hears it (`signal.Wanted`) at once when it grows and 3 s after a layer leaves it, and every layer stays wanted for the first 10 s after the publish, so the publisher's congestion control has run every layer its uplink carries (the top one after its probe, 5–7 s in) and can resume one at once later; the room's clock and each viewer's settings work it out. The whiteboard is never sent video, so a camera with only it in the room pauses 10 s after it's published. Speakers (`speakers.go`, LiveKit's AudioLevel adapted): each voice's RFC 6464 levels over 400 ms windows of RTP time, the list sent whole, loudest first, every 300 ms while it changes (empty once, when everyone's quiet). Then the network (one UDP socket per address on one port, `Network.IP`, when set, the only address offered (unset, every address but virtual interfaces'; a loopback IP offers loopback candidates), virtual interfaces skipped, `External` advertised behind a NAT, full ICE, mDNS off; lksdk's DTLS and ICE timings) and the two roles' pion APIs (`up`: NACK generator, receiver reports, TWCC feedback; `down`: NACK responder of 512 packets, transport-wide sequence numbers; no sender-report interceptor, since down tracks translate the publisher's). The forwarder (`forwarder.go`: one viewer's layer of one video track; a switch waits for the new layer's keyframe (`h264.go`: the packet carrying the SPS) and for the old layer to finish its frame, or 150 ms of its silence, holding the new layer meanwhile; sequence numbers rewritten so the viewer gets one unbroken stream, timestamps passed through since a publication's layers share one base; a late retransmission of the run before a switch still goes out in its gap; a keyframe older than the frame last sent is used anyway, the client showing only newer frames; the downlink's padding takes the sequence numbers after a frame, the run carrying on past them, and the runs before are kept, 64 of them, for their late packets). Each subscriber's downlink (`downlink.go`): pion's GCC on its down connection with a no-op pacer, forwarding immediate (the server's one down API carries the `cc` factory, and each connection takes its estimator by the id pion made it under), its delay-based part followed (loss on its own isn't a link too narrow); free, every video at the layer its settings want, until a queue of 30 ms stands in front of the link for 100 ms with the estimate under 0.9 of what's sent (`resend.go`'s `queue`: the lowest one-way delay the feedback shows over the last 150 ms, over the lowest of 10 s, less what jitter usually keeps that window's lowest over it (its 25th percentile), up to 30 ms; every packet waits in a queue, jitter lets some through, a keyframe's burst drains within the window; GCC alone took a lossy network's jitter for overuse); then limited: each video the largest layer at or under its want that fits the budget (the estimate, for a video starting capped at 1.5× the video sent + 100 kbps, less 40 kbps for each voice sent, at most three), in priority order (a screen, then larger asked areas, then smaller), every video's smallest layer before any is raised, so the lowest-priority one goes off first and the first never does; a layer being sent kept while 3/4 of it fits (all of it while a queue stands); the layers to choose from are those that run and those paused only because nobody wanted them (wanted again, the publisher starts them), measured at the SFU over 2 s, without filler (their published bitrate until a second of it, and while paused); down at once; up only through a probe, 10 s after the last drop and 2 s after a move: padding-only packets after a frame's marker, on the video moving up (or the highest-priority camera sending), rising over 5 s to what moving up adds (the next layer's rate less the current one's), ended by a 10 ms queue standing 50 ms (or GCC's estimate falling while one stands), a failed one holding the video 15 s, doubling to 2 minutes; carried, the video moves up a layer, and what the link carried stands in for the estimate until the link pushes back; free again once every video has had its want for 10 s with no drop. Padding (`padLocked`) is written in the payload (zeros, its length last), so GCC counts it (as the header's `PaddingSize` it counts only the header) and SRTP's pooled buffer's leftovers aren't what's sent. The resend gate (`resend.go`, on the estimator): while the link is saturated (a queue stands in front of it) a NACKed packet is resent no sooner than the feedback's delay and at most twice, the NACK filtered in place before pion's responder reads it (interceptors share one parse of each RTCP read); otherwise every NACK is answered. A publisher's probe filler (an SEI filler payload: `h264.go`'s `filler`) is sent as a one-byte padding packet that keeps its sequence number and marker. `pion_test.go` checks what it relies on pion for, pion to pion; `gcc_test.go` pion's GCC on its sending side; `server_test.go`, `gate_test.go`, `local_test.go`, `video_test.go`, `dynacast_test.go` and `downlink_test.go` (on `harness_test.go`: the server behind httptest on loopback, sfuclient sessions recording what they hear, voices of stand-in frames naming sender and number; `video_test.go`'s camera of three synthetic H.264 layers whose slices name layer and frame, which `dynacast_test.go` has pause the layers its publisher hears nobody wants) the SFU end to end. Pure.

### `internal/ui/boardview`
The board widget: draws a version at the server's `Placement` (or runs the same layout), cards measured by `measure`, frames, arrows, the notes panel, glides, new boxes revealed one by one in the order their ops drew them, `point_at` glow, a camera that fits the board until the user pans or zooms. Pure.

### `internal/ui/transcript`
A transcript panel. The app keeps the transcript's lines (`meeting.addLine`) but doesn't show the panel yet. Pure.

### `internal/ui`
Input `Event`s, the `Widget` contract, the `Host`, focus layers, the `TextField`. Pure.

### `internal/draw`, `internal/geom`, `internal/text`, `internal/theme`
The display list, path geometry, text (go-text shaping, embedded Inter and JetBrains Mono, system fallback; `NewEmbeddedShaper` has none), color tokens. Pure.

### `internal/gfx`
GPU renderer: device and window, the uber-shader pipeline, glyph atlases, `Batch`, `RenderImage` (offscreen). Video frames as images (`video.go`): `PutFrame(id, frame, aspect)` queues an NV12 frame for an image id, and `Prepare` uploads its planes (R8 brightness, R8G8 Cb/Cr) through the `Uploader` (one transfer buffer, grown and cycled; adapted from reel) and converts them with `nv12.frag` into that id's RGBA texture, cropped to `aspect` and mirrored when the frame says, so the window and `RenderImage` both draw it with `draw.List.Image`; `DropFrame` lets it go, `VideoErr` says why the last frames weren't converted.

### `internal/platform`

### `internal/platform/audio`

## Voice
**The path.** Mic → voice processing (AVAudioEngine; the tap delivers 100 ms at a time, released 10 ms a frame) → Opus (20 ms, FEC, DTX) → an `sfuclient` track with the RFC 6464 level → huddled's SFU → each listener: raw RTP → (optional `Sim` interceptor) → jitter buffer → playout mix (10 ms, paced by the speaker) → AVAudioPlayerNode (50 ms queued) → speakers, where voice processing hears it and cancels it from the mic.

**Decisions, and why.**
- **Opus: libopus transpiled to Go (`kazzmir/opus-go`).** pion/opus has no public encoder (its CELT encoder is internal), so encoding had to be libopus either way; for decoding, the measurements below favour libopus too: real concealment and in-band FEC, where pion has neither (ours repeats the last frame, fading). Speech fixture, on the development Mac, `go test ./internal/opus -bench . -run Concealment -v`:

  | | libopus | pion |
  |---|---|---|
  | encode, 20 ms frame (complexity 9) | 0.48 ms (2.4% of a core; 1.3% at complexity 5) | no encoder |
  | decode, 20 ms frame | 44 µs (0.22%) | 55 µs (0.28%) |
  | conceal, 20 ms | 41 µs | 1 µs (repeat-fade) |
  | correlation with the source, 5% loss | 0.913 with FEC, 0.881 PLC only | 0.843 |
  | same, 10% loss | 0.909 with FEC, 0.848 PLC only | 0.762 |
  | lost frames vs the clean decode, 10% | 0.937 with FEC, 0.647 PLC only | -0.10 |

  The transpiled code's moving-stack bug (AGENTS.md, Rules) is contained by the pinned worker; `NewDecoder` can return pion's decoder in one line (`NewDecoderWith(Pion)`) if it ever bites again; `opus.Chosen` can't be switched alone, since it picks the encoder too and pion has none.
- **Our pion interceptors go in first**, nearest the network, ahead of sfuclient's own (NACK, RTCP reports, TWCC), so everything after (RTCP receiver reports, the jitter buffer) sees what they do. The receive-side network simulation is one. sfuclient's `Track` writes the RFC 6464 audio level on every packet (pion's `TrackLocalStaticSample` can't), and the SFU's active-speaker levels show it arrives (`sfuSpeakerLevel` in `voice: track`).
- **Voice processing stays on AVAudioEngine with Go off the real-time thread**, so the mic comes from a tap, and taps come 100 ms at a time on this hardware whatever is asked (4800 frames every 100 ms in the earlier project's logs). Sent as bursts, those look like 80 ms of jitter to every receiver; `paced` releases them evenly instead, which costs nothing the chunking hasn't already (~90 ms on the mic side). That is the largest single latency in the path; lower would need native code on the render thread (an AVAudioSinkNode block or a render callback), which purego can only give us as Go.
- **Every voice's RTP timestamp follows the clock, sent or not** (a mic through DTX and mute, the whiteboard between answers), and **the jitter buffer starts its target over after a second with no packets.** LiveKit, the media server until step 5, padded a track nobody had sent on yet: a new subscriber got 251 frames of 80 bytes over 5.4 s whose timestamps stepped 961 samples every ~21.8 ms, slower than they arrived. That drift alone sets the target to 300 ms, and the 250-arrival window used to keep it there until 250 real packets had come (minutes of the whiteboard's answers); a timestamp that stood still through a silence did the same on every answer. Measured with a recorder in the room (`huddle voice -out`): the whiteboard's answers went from 300 ms to 40 ms. A mic's DTX frame every 400 ms keeps the window, so jitter learned before a pause carries across it (`TestPaddingThenSpeech`).
- **Voice processing doesn't start on every device, so the mic falls back to running without it.** On a USB interface that is both the default mic and speakers (2 in, 2 out, 44.1 kHz) the engine failed to start with `bfmt` (1650879860) in five of six runs of the earlier project and in huddle's first; each failure read the input node's device side as 0 Hz / 0 channels after voice processing was turned on, and the one start that came up read 44.1 kHz / 4 channels there. So start now waits for that format (up to 2 s), `OpenVoice` tries a second engine, and the call then opens the mic without echo cancelling (`NoEchoCancel`, the bar says "use headphones") rather than leaving the person listening only. Whether the wait or the second engine is enough on that interface is the user's run to tell (`audio: voice processing's device is ready`, `audio: the engine didn't start`). The menu's "Echo cancelling: Off" is for headphones.
- **The whiteboard's track sends a silent frame every 400 ms between answers** (`keepAlive` in `internal/server/agent/voice.go`), as a mic sends DTX. LiveKit padded only a track that hadn't sent: before this, an answer that began inside the padding played late, because the padding's arrivals were still in the window. Traced with `huddle voice -arrivals`: the padding's transit climbs about 1 ms a packet (−21 to +96 ms over 1.6 s), then the answer's sits flat at −8 ms, and that answer played at 119 ms (300 ms once, 4 s in). With the keepalive, LiveKit's padding stopped after about 15 frames, and an answer typed 1.5 s after joining played at 41 ms. huddled's SFU pads nothing; the keepalive now keeps listeners' jitter windows going (a jitter buffer starts its target over after a second with no packets). Keepalive frames are 17 bytes and decode to silence; the receiver counts the quiet between them as `skippedMs`, as it does a mic's DTX.
- **The speakers are paced by the player's completions** (`DataRendered`), 10 ms per buffer, 40–50 ms queued; Write gives up waiting after 200 ms and drops past 500 ms queued, so a stopped engine never stalls the mix (the watchdog restarts it).

## Video
- **The path.** Capture (camera, share or pattern: an NV12 `macvideo.Buffer` each frame, the capture's own memory) → `macvideo.Sender` (an encoder per layer, each handed the full picture: VideoToolbox scales it) → `room` (a simulcast track; GCC, the allocator and our pacer decide which layers run at what rate) → huddled's SFU (each receiver gets the layer its `SetSize` fits, within what its downlink carries) → `room` again (assembler → held for lip sync → decoder → newer than the last shown → `video.Latest`) → the window takes the newest frame (Track U's tiles).
- **Layers.** A camera: its picture at up to 1280 wide (1.7 Mbps at 720p), half (450 kbps at 360p) and a quarter (160 kbps at 180p), all at 30 fps. A screen: up to 1920 wide (2.5 Mbps at 1080p) and half (800 kbps), at up to 15 fps. A layer runs from 40% of its bitrate (its floor) to all of it. Keyframes every 5 s at most, and whenever a receiver asks (once per 200 ms per layer).
- **Congestion control.** pion's GCC (`cc` + `gcc.NewSendSideBWE`) gives an estimate; sfuclient hands over the publish connection's estimator inside Join.
  - **pion's GCC climbs while less is sent than it allows** (8% a second, to its maximum: it has no cap at the acknowledged rate as WebRTC's has), so the layers are given at most 1.5× what was sent plus 100 kbps ("usable" in the logs), except while a probe runs, when they're given from GCC's estimate. `huddle video` prints both.
  - **A paused layer comes back through a probe:** the highest running layer is given the paused layer's floor on top, rising over 8 s (4 s while the estimate has never dropped), and pads what its encoder doesn't use with SEI filler payloads (NAL 6, message 3; pion's payloader drops filler-data NAL units, type 12). If GCC drops meanwhile, the probe ends at once and the layer waits 15 s, doubling to 2 minutes; if it carries the floor, the layer starts at its floor. Probing with the paused layer itself was tried first: the SFU switched receivers up to it and back, with keyframes both ways and the voice's jitter delay at 260 ms. Probes wait for 10 s with no drop of 15% or more in GCC's estimate.
  - **A running layer pauses under 70% of its floor** (a paused one comes back only at 115% of it, through a probe): GCC answers one overuse by cutting to 0.85 of what arrived, and a screen's 1080p starting at its floor paused at the first one (its opening keyframe) until the next probe.
  - **Our pacer**, not pion's (one queue for everything, the voice behind keyframes): the voice goes out the moment it's written, video at 1.2× the estimate the layers were given from (the cap; not GCC's estimate, which climbs), counting a layer Dynacast paused at what it ran at: the cap follows what's sent, and with a camera's smaller layers paused a 720p frame alone took 18 ms of its 33 to leave (capture → shown 27–28 ms against 20–22). Retransmissions go out on their own stream (no RTX), so the pacer queues them with that stream's video.
  - **The low-latency rate control isn't used for the room's layers.** Squeezed (a 360p layer at 255 kbps) it drops to 17–20 fps, and given its bitrate back in steps it stays there; the ordinary real-time rate control held 30 fps through the same swings (`TestBitrateSwings`) and encodes in 9.2 ms instead of 8.5 (`video-probe`, 720p, median), with a better worst PSNR (46.1 dB against 31.7).
  - **Dynacast** (step 4a). The SFU tells a publisher which layers anyone is sent (`signal.Wanted`: each viewer's layer, the smaller one its downlink allows while that's limited, and the one its forwarder sends until a switch completes), at once when that grows and 3 s after a layer leaves it; for the first 10 s after a publish every layer is wanted, so the allocator has run every layer the uplink carries (the top one comes after a 4 s probe). A layer nobody wants is given nothing (`macvideo.Sender` then skips its encoder, and starts it again with a keyframe, the time skipped taken out of what VideoToolbox sees: `Encoder.Skipped`), and isn't a failed try. Wanted again, it comes back at its floor at once if it ran at its floor or more before, GCC's estimate leaves 1.15× its floor, and the estimate hasn't dropped since it paused; else it waits for a probe, as a layer the estimate paused does; a layer wanted under a larger one that runs starts at its floor at once (the larger one makes room). Meanwhile the next smaller layer stands in for it (on the SFU, the nearest running layer stands in for its viewers), so a viewer is never left with nothing; with nothing running to carry a probe, the lowest layer needed starts at its floor.
- **Receiving.** Packets are put back together into access units by `room`'s assembler, not pion's samplebuilder, which holds each frame until the next frame's first packet comes (a frame's time; on a still screen share, until something changes). A unit also ends where the next one begins without its marker, at a new timestamp or a keyframe's first packet (the one carrying the SPS): an SFU cuts a frame off when it switches a viewer to another layer at that layer's keyframe, and the cut frame is dropped, not glued to the keyframe. The assembler starts at a keyframe's first packet (else after the first marker); it used to wait for the first marker, which threw away the keyframe a subscription starts with. Frames are shown only when newer than the last shown (RTP time, wrap-aware; more than 5 s behind is a clock that started over): a switch to a layer that lags sends a few frames again from its keyframe, decoded but not shown. A missing packet is waited for 250 ms from when it showed (a later packet came; NACKs usually bring it back); after that the units it touched are dropped (to the next keyframe's first packet, or the packet after the next marker, whichever comes first), and the track waits for a keyframe and asks for one at once (a PLI on the receive connection), again every 300 ms while no packet of one has come. Meanwhile a frame a packet shows isn't a keyframe is dropped at once, holes and all, so the keyframe goes out as soon as it's whole. The wait used to start when the assembler got to the hole: holes never mended then queued one 250 ms wait behind another, and at 20% loss with jitter the video fell 12–23 s behind (step 3b). Through LiveKit (before step 5), a PLI stopped its retransmissions until it forwarded a keyframe (`FlagStopRTXOnPLI`): falling behind, the receiver asked about once a second, and 8.5% of packets were never mended; keeping up, 0.9%. A track shown again after `SetSize(0, 0)` waits for a keyframe and asks for one 100 ms after turning on. On huddled's SFU a viewer joining a running camera shows its first frame about 100–120 ms after the track arrives, at the layer its first settings ask for (step 3's runs); where camera and viewer start together, about 650 ms, the encoder starting meanwhile. It took about 1.1 s at the top layer (0.6 s at 180p) while the assembler waited for the first marker: that threw away the keyframe the SFU starts a subscription with, and the next came only after a PLI.
- **Lip sync.** A video frame is held until the same person's voice, as playout plays it (`jitter.Buffer.Playing`, plus the speaker's delay), reaches the time it was taken. A frame due more than 500 ms ahead is shown at once (the clocks disagree). The two are compared by arrivals: each track keeps when each RTP timestamp would have come with the least delay of the last 2–4 s (`srClock`), and a person's voice and video cross the same network, so that least delay is the same for both. Sender reports are used only while a track has no packets: an SFU's may come seconds in (frames led the voice by 40–55 ms until then), and a video's moves 1–13 ms from one report to the next, since pion stamps a report at the last packet written, after the encoder; the voice's agreed with its arrivals within a millisecond. Both clocks place a voice timestamp a packet (20 ms) late, a packet going out once all of it is captured, and the hold takes that off (`voiceSent`); a video's timestamp, its capture time, is placed late only by its encoding, about what Opus's own delay puts the sound behind. The voice's position comes only from pulls of the stream itself: through DTX silence a jitter buffer's position stands still, and holding against it made frames late by up to 110 ms. A pull's position is counted back from where it ended: one that skips the rest of a silence and plays the next talk spurt began at the silence's position, and with the spurt's first packets arriving at once (behind a narrow uplink) a frame was held 350 ms (step 5). With no voice playing (none sent, or no speakers), frames show as they decode.
- **Camera and share in the app.** `setCamera(true)` asks for the Camera permission if macOS hasn't yet (only from the button), opens the camera picked in the menu (`devicePrefs.Camera`, else the default; asked again while on, it moves to the one picked), waits for its first picture to size the layers, and publishes; the self-view is the camera's own frames (`Mirror` set; the window mirrors your tile either way), and what's sent isn't mirrored. `setShare(true)` shows the system picker (on the main thread, through `performSelectorOnMainThread:`), returns once the share runs (nil when the picker was closed), and the share ends by itself from the menu bar's stop. Both are published again when voice reconnects. Each self-view frame wakes the loop (`platform.Wake`); remote frames wake it through the tiles' poller ("How tiles draw", below).
- **VideoToolbox works through purego.** The output callbacks are made once and shared, keyed by an id in the refcon (purego never frees callbacks). The encoder's callback takes only scalars. The decoder's gets two CMTimes after the image; on arm64 a 24-byte struct arrives as a pointer to a copy, and purego callbacks take no structs, so they're `uintptr`s. Calls that take a CMTime by value (`VTCompressionSessionEncodeFrame`) go through `RegisterLibFunc`, which passes it by pointer as the ABI wants.
- **The encoder scales a frame of another size by itself.** A 720p buffer handed to a 360p encoder comes out 360p and reads right, so every simulcast layer's encoder can take the full-size capture buffer. Track P measured that against `Scaler`: cheaper (`BenchmarkLayers`; Track P's numbers in `history.md`).
- **A decoder given a stream that changes size makes its session again.** 720p, then 360p, then 720p takes 2 rebuilds, and every picture reads right. `VTDecompressionSessionCanAcceptFormatDescription` says no to a new size.
- **Low-latency rate control is offered here** (`kVTVideoEncoderSpecification_EnableLowLatencyRateControl`), with Constrained Baseline. Where it isn't, the encoder is made without it. Where there's no H.264 encoder at all (a CI virtual machine), the tests skip.
- **Loading VideoToolbox prints an AVCaptureDevice deprecation warning** about Continuity Cameras on stderr. It comes from the frameworks, not from us; nothing opens a camera.
- **How tiles draw (Track U).** Each frame the scene takes every video on screen from its source (`Take`: the newest frame if it's newer than the last taken, one copy under the track's lock) and puts it to the renderer (`PutFrame`); `Prepare` uploads the planes and runs `nv12.frag` into that video's RGBA texture (a camera cropped to 16:9, a share whole, your camera mirrored), and the tile draws it with `draw.List.Image` and its rounded corners. Each track hears the pixel size it's shown at when that changes (`SetSize`), and 0, 0 once it's off screen (a share not on stage, a camera behind the "+N" tile). A goroutine looks at the videos on screen every 4 ms (a `Take` with nothing to do) and wakes the loop when one has a new frame, so a call with no video still renders nothing while idle. Tiles appear once anyone's camera is on: a column on the right, or a row along the top when the stage would be narrower than 560; everyone but the whiteboard gets one, initials while their camera is off.
- **Open issues:**
  - **A viewer's downlink on huddle's SFU starts free**, sent what it asks until the link pushes back, and the first overload costs the voice: behind 1 Mbps, the publisher's probe raising the 360p the viewer is sent (its encoder given the bits, not only filler), then its 720p starting or rising to its full rate, queues 35–160 ms before the downlink is limited, and the jitter buffer's step up and its steps back down each cost a second of the speech (0.4–0.6): the run 0.80–0.87 against 0.916 unlimited and the 0.88 line (step 4b; the integration's quiet runs), nothing lost. Moving down within 0.2–0.6 s still scores 0.84–0.87: any queue the SFU can tell from jitter moves the jitter buffer. A limit waits for GCC's estimate to fall under 0.9 of what's sent as well as a 30 ms queue standing, since the queue alone limited a viewer at 10% loss with jitter, and GCC misses a queue that builds slowly (once 3.7 s, the delay at 160 ms). A failed probe costs a step or two more.
  - **Each probe for a paused layer costs the voice 10–30 ms of underrun**; at 1 Mbps the voice scored 0.867–0.916 across runs against a 0.88 line (step 3b's runs, main's build and its own).
  - **A publisher's probe for a paused layer is fragile**: one drop in GCC's estimate ends it, then the layer waits 15 s, doubling. In step 3's load run one camera's 720p never started (its estimate held at 0.9 Mbps), and a screen's 1080p came in one of two runs.
  - **Lip sync assumes a frame's encoding takes about as long as Opus's own delay:** with a 720p encoder 75–90 ms behind capture (a busy Mac), frames showed 49 ms after the beep (step 3b). Sender reports stamped at capture, or the abs-capture-time extension carried through the SFU, would remove the assumption.
  - **The camera's and the share's failure messages are generic** ("The camera wouldn't start"); the details are in the log.

## The ears
A spoken command passes through more than a typed one: the person's track is decoded at 16 kHz; `pcm.Detector` finds each utterance (−45 dBFS, 60 ms of loudness to start, 450 ms of quiet to end, checked every 100 ms whether packets come or not); the utterance (with 300 ms before it) goes to that person's `gemini-3.5-transcribe-live` session, then `audioStreamEnd`; the final comes back; the gate waits 700 ms for more (`ContinueMs`) or, for the key, for the transcript of what was said while held. Measured through the app's mic path: last word → command about 0.9–1.0 s, then the brain's first op about 0.4–0.7 s. A typed command skips all of it.
- **The transcriber's final is usually 0.3–0.5 s after the stream end, and now and then seconds late.** A session sent the next utterance meanwhile folds both into one final (the user's first voice run: a minute of speech in one segment). So `transcribe` takes no more audio until the last utterance's final has come; after 4 s (`finalWait`) the latest interim stands in and the session is replaced. An utterance whose interims had no words (a cough, a noise) gets no final, so it waits 1 s (`noWordsWait`).
- **The app's mic sends DTX: one packet every 400 ms in silence; muted, one when the mute begins (so receivers play silence, not concealment), then none.** The ears read with a 100 ms deadline and feed the detector quiet on each timeout, so the end of speech is found ~450 ms after the last word instead of at the next packet (780–840 ms before). The fixture bots send every packet, so `agent-check` doesn't exercise this; `commands.wav` through `huddle voice -in` does.

## What the models do (live-probe, 2026-10-27)
`make live-probe` (`huddled live-probe [-only brain|transcribe|resume] [-auto-schedule]`) asks the real models; about $0.10 a run. Findings:
- **`gemini-3.8-live` takes commands either way, mid-session.** Ten commands alternating client content (`Session.SendTurn`) and realtime text (`SendText`) in one session all drew: first tool call 0.35–1.0 s after the send. Neither path broke tool calls after the other was used.
- **A whole turn's tool calls come first, in one message, then audio.** On a question the `speak` call arrives at 0.36–0.42 s and the first audio at 0.86–1.3 s, after `speak` is answered. So the agent knows a turn will be spoken before any audio arrives.
- **Answer `speak` `WHEN_IDLE`, never `SILENT`.** A `SILENT` answer to `speak` left the question unanswered, and the model said that answer aloud during a later, unrelated command.
- **Answer successful drawing calls `SILENT`.** Answered `WHEN_IDLE`, a drawing turn starts another generation per answer: one command repeated the same three `add_node` calls six times, and another spoke filler ("<no speech>{pause}"). Answered `SILENT`, every drawing command came as one batch with `turn-complete` at the same moment, and no audio. A rejected call, a read (`get_board`, `transcript`) and `speak` are answered `WHEN_IDLE`, so the model can fix the call, use the read or speak (a `get_board` then a `connect` came 0.37 s apart). So is every call of a turn that called `speak`, and every call of a turn that isn't an auto turn under the `narrate` voice policy.
- **A turn is over at a `turn-complete` with no `WHEN_IDLE` answer outstanding.**
- **`gemini-3.5-transcribe-live` takes 16 kHz s16 PCM in 100 ms chunks and returns text** (setup: TEXT modality, input transcription on). Interim hypotheses (`interimInputTranscription`) come about every 0.5 s while someone talks; the final (`inputTranscription`, then `generation-complete`) comes 0.35–0.58 s after the speech ends when the client sends `audioStreamEnd` at its own VAD's end of speech, and 1.4 s after with the server's VAD alone. The final is punctuated ("Whiteboard, draw our release process: build, then test, then deploy."); "White board" came back as "Whiteboard", but a trailing name sometimes comes without its comma ("…on the board now whiteboard?"). The service aborts a session after about a minute without audio (close 1008, "The operation was aborted"), so the ears close a quiet person's session at 40 s and open another when they speak.
- **Resumption works, with one catch** (`live-probe -only resume`). The server sends one resumable handle per turn, as soon as the turn is taken in. A connection opened with it continues the conversation (it answered "Marmalade." for a code word given on the first connection). But resuming while the old connection is still open fails: close it first. The agent reconnects only between turns, so it closes, then dials.
- **No GoAway came in 33 minutes on one `gemini-3.8-live` connection** with context compression on, unlike the documented ten minutes. The reconnect loop stays (GoAway, a dropped connection), and `agent-check -reconnect-every 8m` replaces the connection on a schedule to exercise it.
